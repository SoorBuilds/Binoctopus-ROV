# August 30th: Designed the V1 Thruster 

Spent the afternoon designing my thruster module (Fusion 360) that houses my motor and propeller
Designed the thruster to be hydrodynamic using smooth arcs and fillets so that water will pass by both the inside and outside with minimal resistance
Designed a weedguard that screws into the thruster itself, this should protect my propeller from getting caught in the weeds later on.

[Lapse Link](https://lapse.hackclub.com/timelapse/PN2ay0xSe8IN)

# September 6th: Designed and tested the V2 thruster

Improved on the design of V1 which I realized would not work. It didn't leave enough room for the motor's nose cone that mounted on top of the propeller, the weedguard threads didn't print well, and the mounting screws of the motor didn't have any place to rest. I fixed these issues in Fusion. Then I tested the new design in my sink (It worked)

# September 6th: Designed the Vectored thruster configuration 

I tried my best to copy the configuration used in the FiFish V6, a very well designed ROV that achieves 6 independent degrees of freedom with only 6 thrusters. It does this by putting the front and back propeller pairs at 45 degrees. It's hard to describe and even harder to reverse engineer. I have a feeling that the thrust mapping software will not be my friend later on.

# September 12th: Spent the whole day working on the shell’s design

Connected all the thrusters to the frame in a high strength yet very flowy and hydrodynamic way. This should allow for easy assembly and a modular, disconnectable thruster system in case one ever breaks. I also designed it to have minimal 3d printing supports. I connected the cable to the rear with a very robust joint that should be able to lift the submarine, and potentially any weeds it gets tangled in.

# September 12th: Finishing touches

Finished designing the shell. I split it up into 3 parts, a rear and a front belly, and a front back so that it should be easy to open up and assemble. I also modeled connection joints that will screw together for a robust connection. Also, I designed a vectored lighting arrangement for 4, 3W LEDs that should allow for a floodlight effect when I'm deep underwater.
